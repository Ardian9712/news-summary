World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026: US Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris that economic pressure on Iran was effective enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer," though the US might still use force if Iran crosses "certain red lines" (New York Times). Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran awaits a US answer to its seven conditions for talks and the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until they are met; Iran's Revolutionary Guards said three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines (Al Jazeera). The US Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over nearly 20 alleged plots in the US and Europe, including one at RAF Fairford in England; Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was moved to a military base after threats (New York Times, Fox News). Ukraine's President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called the first day of trilateral Ukraine-Russia-US talks in Abu Dhabi "constructive," and officials said the sides agreed a large prisoner-of-war exchange. NATO scrambled jets after at least four drones strayed into Baltic states' airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia. UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told leaders at the COP31 climate conference in Antalya, Turkey, that the slow response to climate change had put the world "on a path to destruction." The Democratic Republic of the Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and at least 5,387 deaths, the second-largest on record. The US House voted 211-204 to send an impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it. Israel's new parliament was sworn in without a government after a deadlocked election stripped Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of his majority. British police arrested a 41-year-old US Army officer on suspicion of spying for Russia. Yemen's government said its forces killed or injured about 190 Houthi fighters, unverified; the WHO counts at least 1,771 killed since August and over 300,000 displaced.
